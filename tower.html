How a tower went up.

A scrapbook in eleven pages. Railway tracks in 1970; the tallest free-standing structure in the world by 1975.

What you are looking at

  • RealEvery picture is a photograph taken at the time, by someone who was there, and shared on Wikimedia Commons under an open licence. Most are by Robert Taylor of Stirling, Ontario, who photographed the tower from the day the footings went in. The picture of today is by TooMuchFrixion. Each is credited below.
  • RedrawnThe sketches are made from those photographs in your browser: pencil lines found from the edges in the picture, over a wash of the photograph’s own colours. Nothing is drawn in and no colour is invented. Press “Show the photographs” to see each one as it was taken.
  • RealThe dates and figures, from Wikipedia’s article on the CN Tower: the start on 6 February 1973, the foundation, the slipform and its 6 m a day, the last concrete on 22 February 1974, the helicopter, the topping out at 553.3 m on 2 April 1975, the opening on 26 June 1976, and what came after. The note on each page headed “Not many people know” is from the same article.
  • DrawnThe little tower on each page. It is full at 553.3 m and marked at 381 m when the concrete was done, which are recorded. On the pages in between, how far it is filled is by eye, and the words beside it say only what is known.
  • Not hereThe people who built it. None of these photographs shows the crews, and no account of them was read for this story. The source also gives two counts for the antenna’s sections, 36 and 44, so the story says only that it went up in sections.
