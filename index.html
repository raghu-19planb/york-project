Toronto, redrawn

A film in code & sound ·

In 1834 the city was a strip along the harbour. Its limits were redrawn outward fifty times by 1967, and in 1998 six municipalities became one. Every line is a mapped boundary, added in the order it happened.

How it was made, and what it leaves out

The film is drawn live in your browser from boundary data, not played from a video. Older land is darker; each new piece flashes yellow as it joins. The notes are generated in code, one per annexation.

    What it leaves out: Metropolitan Toronto began in 1954 with thirteen municipalities, but only the six that remained after 1967 are drawn, because boundaries for the other seven were not found. Annexation 48, Victoria Park Avenue, is a strip of road too narrow to show. Outlines are simplified to about 15 metres.

            What was added, and when
              An independent project about Toronto's history. Not affiliated with the City of Toronto or the TTC. Each record links to the source it was checked against. Tkaronto is the Kanien’kéha (Mohawk) word the city’s name comes from. It means “where there are trees standing in the water” (The Canadian Encyclopedia). Inspired by The Madras Project.
