    02 / A city drawn by one maple leaf

    Toronto, Redrawn.

    A maple leaf is the pen. One unbroken line draws the shore of 1802, the city of 1834, every annexation, and the shape Toronto has now.

    · Code & sound

    The line
    A silent glimpse. Play the film to hear the notes.
    How it was made, and what it leaves out

    The film is drawn live in your browser, not played from a video. A maple leaf is the pen: it moves over the ground and leaves one line behind it, about km of it across outlines. Nothing is erased, so later lines cross earlier ones, and at the end the whole drawing is the shape of the city.

    Every solid line is from a source. The shore and creeks are William Chewett’s map of 1802, traced from the georeferenced sheet by the University of Toronto’s Map & Data Library; the straight edges of the sheet are not drawn. The limits are the city’s annexations in the order they happened, then the six municipalities as they stood from 1967. The gold line is today’s shore.

    What is drawing, not data: the dotted strokes are the leaf crossing from one outline to the next, and stand for nothing on a map. The order within a year, where each outline begins, the colours, the leaf and the notes are choices.

      What it leaves out: Metropolitan Toronto began in 1954 with thirteen municipalities, but only the six that remained after 1967 are drawn, because boundaries for the other seven were not found. The film does not date the filling of the waterfront; it only shows the shore of 1802 and the shore now. The outlines are simplified.

      03 / Reconstructing Toronto · 1700 to 1850

      A town taking shape.

      The same ground, followed for a hundred and fifty years: forest, a French fort that burned, ten blocks of York, a battle, and by 1850 a city of thirty thousand on a working harbour.

      Getting around today

      Ride the lines.

      Pick a line and press Ride the line. The lights come on station by station, as on the map above the doors, and the same light moves across Toronto below, so you can see where each stop is.

      What is real here, and what is not

      Real: the lines, their colours, the stations and their order, and the path of each track, from . A line that has opened, closed or changed since then will be wrong here until the data is fetched again. The map under them is the city’s limits and its six old municipalities from the City of Toronto’s open data, and today’s rivers and main roads from OpenStreetMap.

      Worked out: where you can change lines (a station of another line within 300 m), and which part of the city a station is in (the old municipality its point falls inside). Both are rules applied to the data, not the TTC’s own lists. The lake is drawn south of the city’s southern limit, which is close to the shore but is not a survey of it.

      Drawn: the lights, the icons, the chime, and how long a ride takes. This is not a timetable and does not show where any real train is. For service, times and accessibility, use ttc.ca. This site is not affiliated with the TTC.
